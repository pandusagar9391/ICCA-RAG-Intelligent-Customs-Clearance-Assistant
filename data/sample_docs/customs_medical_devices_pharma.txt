# CUSTOMS CLEARANCE REGULATIONS FOR MEDICAL DEVICES AND PHARMACEUTICAL IMPORTS

## 1. REGULATORY OVERVIEW AND GOVERNING AUTHORITIES

Importation of medical devices, drugs, and pharmaceutical products into India is one of the most stringently regulated categories of international trade. It falls under the dual jurisdiction of the **Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO)** under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, and **Indian Customs (CBIC)** under the Ministry of Finance. All regulatory clearances from CDSCO must be electronically endorsed on the ICEGATE portal before customs Out-of-Charge (OOC) can be granted.

Key governing statutes include:
1. **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** (D&C Act) — primary legislation governing import, manufacture, sale, and distribution of drugs.
2. **Medical Devices Rules, 2017** (MDR 2017) — notified under the D&C Act; classifies medical devices into Class A (low risk), Class B (low-moderate risk), Class C (moderate-high risk), and Class D (high risk).
3. **Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985** (NDPS Act) — governs import of narcotic analgesics, controlled psychotropic substances, and precursor chemicals.
4. **Pharmacy Act, 1948** — regulates qualified persons authorized to handle and dispense scheduled drugs.
5. **Foreign Trade Policy (FTP)** — Import policy conditions (Free / Restricted / Prohibited) for drugs and devices under ITC(HS) Schedule I.

## 2. HARMONIZED SYSTEM CLASSIFICATIONS

### 2.1 Medical Devices (Chapter 90)
- **HS 9018**: Instruments and appliances used in medical, surgical, dental, or veterinary sciences, including scintigraphic apparatus, other electro-medical apparatus, and sight-testing instruments.
  - HS 9018.11: Electro-cardiographs (ECG machines)
  - HS 9018.12: Ultrasound scanning apparatus
  - HS 9018.19: Other electro-diagnostic apparatus (MRI, CT scan components)
  - HS 9018.31: Syringes (with or without needles)
  - HS 9018.39: Needles, catheters, cannulae, and similar articles
  - HS 9018.41: Dental drill engines and dental instruments
  - HS 9018.90: Other medical/surgical instruments and apparatus
- **HS 9019**: Mechano-therapy appliances; massage apparatus; physiotherapy apparatus
- **HS 9021**: Orthopaedic appliances; splints; artificial parts of body; hearing aids; pacemakers

### 2.2 Pharmaceutical Products (Chapter 30)
- **HS 3001**: Glands and other organs for organo-therapeutic uses; heparin and its salts
- **HS 3002**: Blood fractions, antisera, vaccines, immunological products, human blood
- **HS 3003**: Medicaments (mixed), not in measured doses or retail packing
- **HS 3004**: Medicaments in measured doses (tablets, capsules, injections) for retail sale — **most common pharmaceutical import HS code**
  - HS 3004.10: Medicaments containing penicillin or streptomycin
  - HS 3004.20: Medicaments containing antibiotics
  - HS 3004.31: Medicaments containing insulin
  - HS 3004.50: Medicaments containing vitamins and other nutritional preparations
  - HS 3004.90: Other medicaments for retail sale (oncology, cardiovascular, CNS drugs)
- **HS 3006**: Pharmaceutical goods — sterile surgical sutures, contrast agents, dental cements

## 3. MANDATORY IMPORT LICENCE AND REGISTRATION REQUIREMENTS

### 3.1 CDSCO Import Licence (Form 10)
Under Section 12 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, **no drug or cosmetic shall be imported into India** except under and in accordance with a licence granted by the **Licensing Authority, CDSCO**. The import licence (Form 10) must specify:
- Name and address of the importer
- Name, strength, and pharmaceutical form of the drug
- Name and address of the foreign manufacturer
- Port of entry
- Quantity authorized for import (may be quantity-restricted or time-limited)

**Exemptions from Import Licence**:
- Drugs imported for personal use not exceeding 100 average doses.
- Drugs imported as samples for testing (up to 10 units), subject to a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from CDSCO.
- Drugs imported by research institutions under a specific research import permit.

### 3.2 Free Sale Certificate (FSC)
The importing entity must produce a **Free Sale Certificate (FSC)** or **Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (CPP)** issued by the competent authority of the country of manufacture, confirming that:
- The product is freely marketed and sold in the country of origin.
- The manufacturing facility complies with WHO Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP).
- The product has marketing authorization from the national regulatory authority.

### 3.3 Medical Device Registration (MDR 2017)
Medical devices are classified under MDR 2017 based on risk:
- **Class A and Class B** devices: Registration required with the State Licensing Authority (SLA).
- **Class C and Class D** devices: Central Licence required from CDSCO (Form MD-14 or Form MD-15).
- Importers must hold a **Certificate of Registration** (Form MD-19) issued by CDSCO for each device model and manufacturer.
- **Notified Bodies**: Devices must bear CE marking (European conformity) or FDA 510(k) clearance, or equivalent recognized international certification.

### 3.4 NDPS (Narcotic and Psychotropic) Import NOC
Drugs listed under Schedule of the NDPS Act, 1985 (opioids, benzodiazepines, amphetamines, barbiturates, and their precursors listed under Tables I and II of the United Nations Convention Against Illicit Traffic) require:
- **Import Permit** issued by the Narcotics Commissioner, Gwalior under the NDPS Rules, 1985.
- **Port-specific Authorization**: The import permit specifies the port of entry, authorized quantity in metric units, consignee details, and validity period (typically 90 days from issue).
- **Post-Importation Report**: Importer must file a post-importation utilization report with the Narcotics Commissioner within 30 days of clearance.

## 4. MANDATORY DOCUMENT CHECKLIST FOR MEDICAL DEVICE AND PHARMA IMPORTS

| # | Document | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bill of Entry (Form I) | Mandatory electronic filing on ICEGATE; HS code accuracy critical |
| 2 | CDSCO Import Licence (Form 10) | Mandatory for all drugs under D&C Act, 1940 |
| 3 | Free Sale Certificate (FSC) / CPP | Certifies product freely marketed in country of origin; WHO GMP compliance |
| 4 | Certificate of Analysis (COA) | Batch-specific laboratory analysis report from manufacturer |
| 5 | Medical Device Registration Certificate | CDSCO registration (Form MD-19) for Class C/D devices; SLA for A/B |

| 7 | Packing List | Temperature-controlled packaging details (Cold Chain: 2–8°C for biologics) |
| 8 | Bill of Lading / Airway Bill | Transport document with cold chain handling instructions if applicable |
| 9 | NDPS Import Permit | Required for all Schedule I & II narcotic/psychotropic substances |
| 10 | Certificate of Origin | Required for FTA duty concessions; Form AI for ASEAN, AHL for Australia |
| 11 | Manufacturer's GMP Certificate | WHO-GMP or equivalent; must be valid and authenticated |
| 12 | Stability Data Report | Required for temperature-sensitive biologics and vaccines |

## 5. CUSTOMS DUTY STRUCTURE FOR MEDICAL AND PHARMACEUTICAL GOODS

### 5.1 Medical Devices (Chapter 90)
- **Basic Customs Duty (BCD)**: 7.5% for most medical devices (HS 9018, 9019, 9021).
- **Exemptions**: Life-saving medical devices specifically listed in Customs Notification No. 12/2012 attract Nil BCD (e.g., pacemakers HS 9021.90, Artificial Kidneys, Cochlear implants).
- **IGST**: 12% on all medical devices.
- **Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS)**: 10% on BCD (Nil if BCD is Nil).

### 5.2 Pharmaceutical Products (Chapter 30)
- **BCD**: 10% for most pharmaceutical formulations (HS 3004).
- **Life-saving drugs**: Nil BCD on specified drugs listed under Customs Notification for anti-cancer, anti-HIV/AIDS, and rare disease drugs.
- **IGST**: 12% on most drugs; 5% on specified drugs for life-threatening diseases.
- **SWS**: 10% of BCD where applicable.

### 5.3 Preferential Duty under Trade Agreements
- **ASEAN-India CECA**: Concessions available for pharmaceutical formulations originating from ASEAN member states.
- **India-Japan CEPA (IJCEPA)**: Duty concessions for specific medical device HS lines from Japan.
- **India-Korea CEPA**: Preferential rates for Korean-origin ophthalmic goods and medical imaging equipment.

## 6. COLD CHAIN AND SPECIAL HANDLING REQUIREMENTS

For biologics, vaccines, blood products (HS 3002), and temperature-sensitive pharmaceutical formulations:
1. **Cold Chain Integrity**: Cargo must be transported in validated temperature-controlled packaging. Importers must provide a **Temperature Excursion Report** from the carrier if any deviation from 2–8°C (or -20°C for frozen biologics) occurred during transit.
2. **Priority Clearance**: Customs authorities grant priority clearance processing for time/temperature-sensitive pharmaceutical consignments under CBIC Circular.
3. **Cold Storage at CFS**: The Container Freight Station (CFS) must have approved cold storage chambers. Importer must pre-book cold storage and intimate the custodian 24 hours prior to vessel berthing.
4. **Viability Window**: CDSCO examination of biologics must be completed within 48 hours of arrival. Customs examination of perishable pharmaceutical goods must not exceed 72 hours.

---

## 7. EXAMINATION AND TESTING PROTOCOL

1. **CDSCO Port Laboratory Testing**: CDSCO designated port laboratory analysts draw random samples (minimum 3 samples per batch) for identity, potency, purity, sterility, and pyrogen testing as per Indian Pharmacopoeia (IP) standards.
2. **Provisional Clearance**: Importers may request provisional release of non-sterile, non-injectible drug formulations against a bank guarantee covering estimated duty differential, pending laboratory report.
3. **Quarantine for Sterile Products**: Sterile injectables, ophthalmic preparations, and biologics must remain in quarantine storage until CDSCO test report certifies sterility and endotoxin compliance.
4. **Rejections**: If CDSCO test reveals non-compliance with IP or USP/BP specifications, the entire batch is marked for mandatory re-export or supervised destruction under customs supervision. No re-export of rejected biologics is permitted to prevent misuse.

---

## 8. PENALTIES FOR NON-COMPLIANCE

- Importation without valid CDSCO Import Licence: Seizure under Section 12 of D&C Act + penalty up to INR 5 Lakh + imprisonment up to 3 years.
- Import of spurious or adulterated drugs: Imprisonment up to 10 years under Section 27(b) of D&C Act.
- NDPS violation: Mandatory minimum imprisonment of 10 years extendable to 20 years, plus INR 1 Lakh to INR 2 Lakh fine under Section 23 of NDPS Act, 1985.
