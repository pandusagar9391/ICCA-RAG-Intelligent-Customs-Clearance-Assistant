# COMPREHENSIVE COMPLIANCE AND CLEARANCE GUIDE FOR IMPORTATION OF INDUSTRIAL MACHINERY AND COMPUTING HARDWARE

## 1. REGULATORY OVERVIEW AND SCOPE
This operational compliance guide governs the statutory customs clearance procedures, document requirements, valuation rules, and technical compliance obligations for importing industrial machinery and automatic data processing equipment. This guide specifically applies to goods classified under the following Harmonized System (HS) chapters and headings:
- **HS 8429**: Self-propelled bulldozers, angledozers, graders, levellers, scrapers, mechanical shovels, excavators, shovel loaders, tamping machines, and road rollers.
- **HS 8471**: Automatic data processing machines and units thereof; magnetic or optical readers, machines for transcribing data onto data media in coded form, and machines for processing such data.
- **HS 8479**: Machines and mechanical appliances having individual functions, not specified or included elsewhere in Chapter 84 (industrial robotics, automated manufacturing equipment, dosing and mixing systems).

All commercial imports must strictly comply with the Customs Act, 1962, the Foreign Trade Policy (FTP), Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) mandatory conformity orders, and Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) procedural handbooks.

---

## 2. STATUTORY DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
Importers or their authorized Customs Brokers (CB) must assemble and file the following primary documentation package through the ICEGATE electronic data interchange (EDI) portal:

1. **Bill of Entry (BOE)**:
   - *Form I (White)*: Bill of Entry for Home Consumption (direct customs clearance upon payment of all duties).
   - *Form II (Yellow)*: Into-Bond Bill of Entry for Warehousing (goods stored in bonded warehouse under Section 59 without immediate duty payment).
   - *Form III (Green)*: Ex-Bond Bill of Entry for Home Consumption from warehouse (clearance of goods from bonded warehouse under Section 68 upon duty payment).
2. **Commercial Invoice**: Must include invoice number, date, Incoterms 2020 (FOB, CIF, CFR), currency of invoice, itemized description, brand, model, unit price, manufacturer name, country of origin, and country of consignment.
3. **Packing List**: Detailed container-wise, crate-wise breakdown indicating gross weight, net weight, dimensions, individual machine part numbers, and serial numbers.
4. **Transport Document**:
   - Master and House Bill of Lading (MBL/HBL) for ocean shipments.
   - Master and House Airway Bill (MAWB/HAWB) for air freight consignments.
5. **Certificate of Origin (COO)**: Non-preferential COO issued by an accredited Chamber of Commerce, or Preferential COO (e.g., ASEAN-India FTA, CEPA, CECA) to claim concessional customs duty benefits.
6. **Import Export Code (IEC)**: Valid 10-digit alphanumeric IEC linked with the importer's PAN and authorized dealer (AD) code registered at the customs clearance port.

## 3. SPECIAL REGULATORY CERTIFICATES AND TECHNICAL MANDATES

### 3.1 Second-Hand Machinery and Chartered Engineer Certificate (CEC)
Importation of refurbished, second-hand, or pre-used capital machinery under HS 8429 and HS 8479 is strictly subject to the following prerequisites:
- **Chartered Engineer Inspection Certificate (CEC)**: Must be issued by an accredited, independent Chartered Engineer or recognized inspection and certification agency from the exporting country.
- The CEC must explicitly certify:
  1. Manufacturer name, year of initial manufacture, and serial numbers of the machines.
  2. Current physical condition, operational status, and details of any major repairs or overhauls performed.
  3. Original invoice value when newly manufactured and current assessed depreciated CIF value.
  4. Minimum remaining residual operational life of the machinery (must be at least 5 years).
  5. Negative declaration confirming that the machinery contains no chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), ozone-depleting substances, or hazardous electronic scrap prohibited under MoEFCC guidelines.

### 3.2 Electronics & IT Goods Compliance (BIS / CRO) for HS 8471
- Automatic data processing machines (servers, workstations, microcomputers, laptop computers under HS 8471) must be registered under the **Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS)** of the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS).
- Every imported machine and packaging unit must visibly display the Standard Mark alongside the unique BIS Registration Number (R-XXXXXXXX) and "IS 13252 (Part 1): 2010" compliance mark.
- Consignments arriving without valid BIS registration are liable to immediate confiscation or re-export at the importer's sole expense.

### 3.3 Wireless Planning and Coordination (WPC) Approval
- Machinery or computing units containing integrated radio frequency modules, Wi-Fi (802.11a/b/g/n/ac/ax), or Bluetooth interfaces require an Equipment Type Approval (ETA) from the WPC Wing of the Ministry of Communications prior to customs out-of-charge.

## 4. CUSTOMS DUTY COMPUTATION METHODOLOGY

Customs duties on imported industrial machinery and electronics are computed based on the Assessable Value (AV) determined under Section 14 of the Customs Act, 1962, read with the Customs Valuation (Determination of Value of Imported Goods) Rules, 2007.

### Duty Structure Components:
1. **Assessable Value (AV)** = CIF Value (Cost + Insurance + Actual/Notional Freight) converted to local currency at the CBIC notified exchange rate.
2. **Basic Customs Duty (BCD)** = Assessable Value $\times$ Applicable BCD Rate (e.g., 7.5% for HS 8429/8479; 0% to 10% for HS 8471).
3. **Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess (AIDC)** = Imposed on specific tariff lines where notified.
4. **Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS)** = 10% on the aggregate amount of BCD and AIDC:
   $$\text{SWS} = (\text{BCD} + \text{AIDC}) \times 10\%$$
5. **Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST)** = Imposed under Section 3(7) of the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 on the aggregate base:
   $$\text{IGST Base} = \text{Assessable Value} + \text{BCD} + \text{AIDC} + \text{SWS}$$
   $$\text{IGST} = \text{IGST Base} \times \text{Applicable IGST Rate (typically 18\%)}$$
6. **Total Customs Duty Payable** = $\text{BCD} + \text{AIDC} + \text{SWS} + \text{IGST}$.

### Worked Calculation Example:
- **Item**: Industrial Automated Packaging Machine (HS Code 8479.89.99)
- **CIF Assessable Value**: INR 5,000,000
- **BCD Rate**: 7.5%
- **AIDC Rate**: 0%
- **SWS Rate**: 10% of BCD
- **IGST Rate**: 18%

*Step-by-step computation:*
1. Assessable Value = INR 5,000,000.00
2. $\text{BCD} = 5,000,000 \times 7.5\% = \text{INR } 375,000.00$
3. $\text{AIDC} = 5,000,000 \times 0\% = \text{INR } 0.00$
4. $\text{SWS} = (375,000 + 0) \times 10\% = \text{INR } 37,500.00$
5. Aggregate Base for IGST = $5,000,000 + 375,000 + 37,500 = \text{INR } 5,412,500.00$
6. $\text{IGST} = 5,412,500 \times 18\% = \text{INR } 974,250.00$
7. **Total Customs Duty Payable** = $375,000 + 37,500 + 974,250 = \text{INR } 1,386,750.00$
*(Effective duty rate = 27.735% of Assessable Value)*

## 5. CONCESSIONAL DUTY BENEFIT SCHEMES
Importers of capital machinery may avail duty exemption or deferral under the following schemes:
1. **EPCG Scheme (Export Promotion Capital Goods)**: Allows import of new capital goods at 0% customs duty, subject to an export obligation equivalent to 6 times the duty saved, to be fulfilled within 6 years.
2. **MOOWR Scheme (Manufacture and Other Operations in Warehouse Regulations)**: Allows import of capital machinery without payment of BCD and IGST when deployed in a bonded manufacturing warehouse. Duty is deferred until finished products are cleared for domestic consumption.

---

## 6. INSPECTION, DOCK EXAMINATION, AND OUT-OF-CHARGE (OOC)
1. **First Check Assessment**: Ordered when customs officers require physical examination of goods prior to assessment of duty (frequent for second-hand machinery and unbranded electronics). The shed appraiser verifies machinery specifications, model numbers, rating plates, and residual life against the CEC.
2. **Second Check Assessment**: Standard procedure where assessment and duty payment precede physical cargo examination at the docks.
3. **Out-of-Charge (OOC)**: Issued by the proper officer under Section 47 of the Customs Act once all duties are settled, compliance certificates verified, and dock examination reports satisfied. The custodian will only release cargo upon verification of electronic OOC on ICEGATE.
